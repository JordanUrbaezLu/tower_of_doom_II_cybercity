## THE FIRST BRIEF ASKED FOR THE WRONG THING

The first version of this doc asked for a conventional contrast S-curve —
deepen the darks, brighten the highlights. That is what "enhance the contrast"
means as an instruction, and it would have made the actual complaint **worse**.

**The card's content is not low-contrast against the card. It is low-contrast
against the panel it sits in, and it is DARK.** Measured
(`node tools/card_contrast_report.js --regions`, WCAG ratios between each
band's ink and the field behind it):

| band | median | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| title plate (white on amber) | 9.8:1 | already excellent |
| rarity band (white on the rarity colour) | 11.8:1 | already excellent |
| effect text (REGULAR / ULTIMATE) | 11.1:1 | fine |
| effect text (SUPER, violet on navy) | ~6.1:1 | half the other two |
| the illustration inside the screen panel | see below | **the problem** |

The illustrations are drawn in **dark charcoal and dark navy on a dark navy
panel**. Opened and looked at: the RECOIL rifle, the HANDLING rifle and the
SPRINT boot are near-silhouettes — the object's whole body sits inside the
panel field's own luminance band and only its black outline separates it.
Deepening the darks pushes those bodies *further* into the field.

So the instrument is inverted: **hold the panel field where it is and lift the
mid-tones, where all the artwork lives.** Control points, applied to R, G and B
(alpha untouched):

```
0:0   32:30   60:88   110:145   170:198   255:255
```

Below 32 nothing moves (the deep navy field, the black outlines, the card
body). From 32 to 110 the slope is about 2.0 — that band is the artwork, and it
roughly doubles in separation from the field. Above 170 it eases back so
nothing clips.

**Calibrated by rendering it and looking, not by reasoning about it.** Five
cards were curved and opened at full size before this was written. The RECOIL
rifle and the SPRINT boot read clearly; the GIANT SLAYER robot and the DAMAGE
rocket, which were already fine, do not blow out; the chassis still reads as
the same card.

| card | panel INK coverage | effect text | rarity band |
|---|---|---|---|
| RECOIL regular | 7.1% → 24.7% | 11.4:1 → 13.2:1 | 12.3:1 → 13.6:1 |
| HANDLING regular | 8.7% → 25.5% | 11.1:1 → 13.0:1 | 12.3:1 → 13.6:1 |
| SPRINT super | 11.0% → 34.5% | 6.1:1 → 8.2:1 | 4.7:1 → 9.6:1 |
| DAMAGE regular | 11.7% → 25.7% | 11.3:1 → 13.2:1 | 12.3:1 → 13.6:1 |

"INK coverage" is the share of the screen panel that is more than 25 luminance
steps from the panel's own field — i.e. how much of that panel is *drawing*
rather than *background*. On the worst cards it roughly triples. That is the
number that means "the content is easier to see".

**Two metrics were built and discarded before that one, and both PASSED
HONESTLY while measuring the wrong thing** — the repo's standing trap
(`check-passes-wrong-question`). An Otsu ink/field split of the panel said
every illustration was 7:1 or better, because it counted the white sparkles and
cyan speed lines as "ink" and everything else as "field"; a
brightest-second-mode measure reported "panel L31 vs icon L42" for *every* card
including the bright orange rocket, because the runner-up mode is the lit
corner of the panel's own vignette. Both were real numbers answering a question
nobody asked. The third measure was only trusted after its ranking was checked
against the images by eye. Note that the OTSU art ratio goes slightly DOWN
under the new curve (9.1 → 8.6 on DAMAGE) — that is the discarded metric
behaving as expected when mid-tones rise, not a regression.

**On-screen size.** The card is drawn at 234×351 in the 1280×720 virtual canvas
(`tod_upgrade.lua` CARD_Y0/CARD_Y1, +10% since 2026-08-27), i.e. 351×527 real
pixels at 1080p. The pack's preview folder is the 1080p size.

# UPGRADE CARDS — make the content easier to see (114 images, tone only)

## What this is

114 Black Ops 3 zombies upgrade cards, all one family: same 768 × 1152 canvas,
same chassis (rounded navy body, amber title plate, a "screen" art panel
holding the illustration, a rarity band, a dark plate carrying the effect text,
level pips), same outlined display face. They are drawn at **351 × 527 px on a
1080p screen** (234 × 351 at 720p) — see `preview_onscreen_351x527/`, that is
what the player actually sees.

**The problem: the illustration inside the screen panel is hard to see.** Most
of the icons are drawn in dark charcoal or dark navy, and the panel behind them
is also dark navy — so the object's body sits in the same tonal band as its
background and only the black outline separates it. Look at
`reference/i_tod_card_recoil_regular.png` and
`reference/i_tod_card_sprint_super.png`: the rifle and the boot are nearly
silhouettes. Compare `reference/i_tod_card_giant_slayer_super.png`, whose robot
is a mid-grey and reads instantly. That difference is the whole brief.

**This is NOT a normal "increase the contrast" job, and doing it that way would
make it worse.** The usual contrast move deepens the darks — which would push
those already-dark illustrations further into the already-dark panel. What is
needed is the opposite in the shadows: **leave the darkest tones alone and lift
the mid-tones, where all the artwork lives.**

**Deliver all 114 files, same names, in one zip.** Every card gets the SAME
adjustment — this is a set, and it must still match itself afterwards.

## The adjustment — exact

Apply this curve to the **red, green and blue channels equally** (linear
between the control points), and **leave the alpha channel untouched**:

| input | output |
|---|---|
| 0 | 0 |
| 32 | 30 |
| 60 | 88 |
| 110 | 145 |
| 170 | 198 |
| 255 | 255 |

What it does: below 32 nothing moves, so the deep navy panel field, the black
outlines and the card body stay exactly as they are. Between 32 and 110 the
slope is about 2×, so the artwork — which lives almost entirely in that band —
lifts away from the field it is sitting on. Above 170 it eases off so nothing
clips to white.

**If you can run code, run exactly this** (Python, Pillow + NumPy) with the
zip's `reference/` folder beside it — it produces the whole delivery:

```python
from PIL import Image
import numpy as np, glob, os

# control points (input -> output), applied to R, G and B; alpha untouched
pts_in  = [0, 32,  60, 110, 170, 255]
pts_out = [0, 30,  88, 145, 198, 255]
lut = np.interp(np.arange(256), pts_in, pts_out).round().astype(np.uint8)

os.makedirs("out", exist_ok=True)
for f in sorted(glob.glob("reference/*.png")):
    im = Image.open(f).convert("RGBA")
    a = np.array(im)
    a[..., :3] = lut[a[..., :3]]          # RGB through the curve
    Image.fromarray(a, "RGBA").save(os.path.join("out", os.path.basename(f)))
print("done")
```

If you work in an editor instead: Curves, RGB (composite) channel, the six
points above, applied to each file as-is, saved as PNG-32 with the alpha
channel preserved. Set the curve once and apply it to every file — **no
per-file judgement, no Auto Levels, no Auto Contrast, no Auto Tone** (each of
those picks a different stretch per image and the set stops matching).

## Hard rules

- Canvas exactly **768 × 1152 px**, PNG, RGBA (32-bit with alpha), one file
  per reference, **same filename**. No resampling, no crop, no padding.
- **The alpha channel is byte-identical to the reference.** The transparent
  rounded corners and the soft coloured glow halo around the SUPER, ULTIMATE
  and TIER cards live in the alpha — they must not change. Only R, G and B move.
- **Same curve on every file.** No per-image tuning, no auto-anything.
- **Nothing is redrawn.** No regeneration, no re-typing of the words, no
  sharpening, no noise, no denoise, no blur, no separate hue or saturation
  adjustment on top. A small saturation rise from the RGB curve itself is
  expected and fine; the icons should not change hue.
- The deepest tones must NOT get darker. If your result has a deeper black
  point or a darker panel field than the reference, the curve went in
  backwards — that is the one failure mode this job has.
- Overlaying any delivered file on its reference must show perfect
  registration: every edge, letter and pip in the same place, only the tone
  different.

## What "done" looks like

Open the delivered `i_tod_card_recoil_regular.png` next to its reference. The
rifle should read as a distinct object against the panel instead of a
silhouette, while the panel itself stays dark and the black outlines stay
black. Same for the SPRINT boot. Nothing else about the card should look
different.

Measured, before → after, on four samples:

| card | how much of the panel is drawing rather than background | effect-text contrast |
|---|---|---|
| RECOIL regular | 7.1% → 24.7% | 11.4:1 → 13.2:1 |
| HANDLING regular | 8.7% → 25.5% | 11.1:1 → 13.0:1 |
| SPRINT super | 11.0% → 34.5% | 6.1:1 → 8.2:1 |
| DAMAGE regular | 11.7% → 25.7% | 11.3:1 → 13.2:1 |

If your result moves these two or three times as far, the curve was applied
twice.

      two unharmed

      the same file in `preview_onscreen_351x527/`

## Do NOT

- Do not apply a normal contrast boost. Deepening the shadows is exactly
  backwards here and will make the dark illustrations harder to see.
- Do not regenerate, repaint or "improve" any card — this is a tone curve on
  the existing pixels, nothing more.
- Do not change the alpha channel, the canvas size, or the filenames.
- Do not run Auto Levels / Auto Contrast / Auto Tone, or tune files
  individually.
- Do not add sharpening, grain, glow, vignette or a colour grade.
- Do not deliver a partial set — the 114 ship together.

## Install (when the drop lands)

1. `.\tools\make_art_pack.ps1 -Inspect 'files (N).zip'` — headers, md5 vs the
   repo copies, nested zips expanded. Every row should read
   `REPLACES repo copy`.
2. `node tools/card_contrast_report.js --compare <extracted folder>` — the
   "nothing broke" gate. FAILS on any size or colour-type change, on any alpha
   difference, or on a file whose overall contrast went DOWN.
3. `node tools/card_contrast_report.js --regions --from <extracted folder>` —
   the "it actually worked" read, and the one that matters for this job.
   Compare against the shipped set (`--regions` with no `--from`): panel INK
   coverage should roughly triple on the faint cards (RECOIL regular 7.1%,
   HANDLING regular 8.7%, DRAW CUT regular 7.7% are the three lowest today) and
   the SUPER cards' effect text should climb from ~6:1 toward the ~11:1 the
   other two rarities already have. **The Otsu `art` ratio dropping slightly is
   EXPECTED** — that is the discarded metric, and lifting mid-tones compresses
   it by construction. Do not read it as a regression.
4. LOOK at RECOIL regular, SPRINT super, GIANT SLAYER super and a TIER card at
   full size beside the repo copies.
5. Copy all 114 into `source_data/tod_ui_images/_images/` (same names: no GDT /
   zone / Lua edits).
6. FULL build. Proof: NEW content-hash `.iwi` for a sample of the cards with
   fresh mtimes, beside an untouched control (`i_tod_card_base` or
   `i_tod_frame_regular` keep their old files).
7. Flip this STATUS to SHIPPED with the build version; CHANGELOG entry; the
   player-facing note is one line ("upgrade cards: the artwork reads more
   clearly").

## If it is still not enough

The lever is one line: steepen the 32→110 segment (e.g. `60:100, 110:158`) and
re-pack. Everything else in this doc stays. The opposite mistake — reaching for
a conventional contrast boost — is recorded at the top of this doc so it does
not get made twice.
